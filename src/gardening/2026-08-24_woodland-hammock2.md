This is the second post in a series about converting a shady back corner of the yard into a woodland.

Here we are covering how the first three steps in the to-do list went:
1. Order mulch
2. Pull up the grass carpet
3. Put down the mulch

## Ordering Mulch
The process was straightforward. I used Google Maps to see what mulch places were in my local area, and found out through their websites that I needed to figure out the amount of mulch in cubic yards (yd<sup>3</sup>). I measured the length and width of the woodland space then used a calculator on one of the vendor's websites to figure out how many cubic yards I would need, with a depth of 2 inches of mulch. The calculator told me **4yd<sup>3</sup>**. I decided to triple it just to be safe, so I could use more mulch if needed and also have mulch for other areas I wanted to work on. This gave me a total of **12yd<sup>3</sup>** to order.

Then came the actual ordering part. I chose a vendor based on price - both the mulch and the delivery fees. Then I just had to call and put in my order for **12yd<sup>3</sup>** double grind brown mulch. It was delivered the next day! Now it was time to start removing all the greenery.

## Pulling Up the Plants
I called it a grass carpet earlier, but there wasn't much grass. It was mostly Lady's Thumb with a few dandelions and other ground-hugging plants mixed in. I did not take the time to ID them all, I was on a rampage. The Lady's Thumb is really easy to pull up, progress was quick. It took about 5 days to clear the whole area, spending about 2 hours each morning. All the plants were tossed into the wheelbarrow as I pulled them out, and when the wheelbarrow was full, I would dump it in an area where it is okay for them to cold compost. This part of the project was the easiest but also tiring. I basically just crawled in the dirt ripping up plants while listening to music. You want to try to get every little green thing, which I didn't do perfectly. I fully expect to be weeding the area in a few weeks to get the stragglers. There are also alternatives out there to clearing the space, they just weren't options for me with the trees and my timeframe. I had first gotten some black tarps and put those down to smother the plants, it would have only taken a few weeks with the local climate. But I found out later that day that the trees could get damaged because their roots would be smothered with everything else and decided to just hand-clear the whole area.

## Put Down the Mulch
After two days of pulling up plants, I started putting down mulch to cover the areas I had just cleared. I found out the hard way that a shovel is not the right tool for gathering mulch into the wheelbarrow, and went to the hardware store for a pitchfork. Much better!

![All Pulled Up and Partially Mulched](/images/posts/hammock-hangout/IMG_6358.jpg "All Pulled Up and Partially Mulched")
<em>All Pulled Up and Partially Mulched</em>

This is how the rest of the week went. I would pull up plants for an hour or two, then put down mulch for an hour. For the trees, I learned you want to taper down to just about nothing right around their trunk, so the mulch doesn't keep the bark moist. The tradeoff here is that I am signed up for weeding the trees for a while.
