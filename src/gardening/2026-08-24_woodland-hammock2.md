This is the second post in a series about converting a shady back corner of the yard into a woodland.

Here we are covering how the first three steps in the to-do list went:
1. Order mulch
2. Pull up the grass carpet
3. Put down the mulch

## Ordering Mulch
The process was straightforward. I used Google Maps to see what mulch places were in my local area, and found out through their websites that I would need to know the amount of mulch in cubic yards (yd<sup>3</sup>). I measured the length and width of the woodland space then used a calculator on one of the vendor's websites to figure out how many cubic yards I would need, with a depth of 3in of mulch. The calculator told me **4yd<sup>3</sup>**. I decided to triple it just to be safe, so I could use more mulch if needed and also have mulch for other areas I wanted to work on. This gave me a total of **12yd<sup>3</sup>** to order.

Then came the actual ordering part. I chose a vendor based on price of the mulch and the delivery fees. Then I just had to call and put in my order for **12yd<sup>3</sup>** double grind brown mulch. It was delivered the next day! Now it was time to pivot to removing all the greenery.

## Pulling Up the Plants
I called it a grass carpet in the to-do list, but there wasn't really any grass. It was mostly _identify plant here_ with a few dandelions and other ground-hugging plants mixed in. I did not take the time to ID them, I was on a rampage. The _identify plant here_ is really easy to pull out, progress was quick. It took about 5 days to clear the whole area, spending about 2 hours each morning. All the plants were tossed into the wheelbarrow as I pulled them out, and when the wheelbarrow was full, I would dump it in an area where it is okay for them to cold compost. This part of the project was the easiest but also tiring. I basically just crawled in the dirt ripping up plants while listening to music. There are alternatives out there to clearing the space, they just weren't options for me with the trees and the timeframe. I had first gotten some black tarps and put those down to smother the plants, it would have only taken a few weeks with the southeastern August heat. But I found out later that day that the trees could get damaged because their roots would be smothered with everything else.

## Put Down the Mulch
After two days of pulling up plants, I started putting down mulch to cover the areas I had just cleared. I found out the hard way that a shovel is not the right tool for gathering mulch into the wheelbarrow, and got a pitchfork. Much better!

![All Pulled Up and Partially Mulched](/images/posts/hammock-hangout/IMG_6358.JPG "All Pulled Up and Partially Mulched")
<em>All Pulled Up and Partially Mulched</em>

This is how the rest of the project went. I would pull up plants for an hour or two, then put down mulch for an hour. For the trees, I learned you want to taper down to just about nothing right around their trunk, so the mulch doesn't keep the bark moist. The tradeoff here is that I am signed up for weeding the trees for a long time.
After two weeks, some of the taproot plants, like dandelions, had returned and were growing through the mulch, so I pulled them out and added 2 more inches of mulch to the specific areas the plants appeared.
